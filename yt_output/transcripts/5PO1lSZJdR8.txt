We know that GLP-1 is really effective in helping people who are overweight or obese lose weight. But what does a person actually feel when taking them? It is worth knowing that these drugs work by slowing down stomach emptying and regulating the area of the brain responsible for feelings of satiety and satisfaction. So, one of the sensations is a decrease in appetite, as you feel fuller faster and last longer. One person I interviewed described it as finally understanding what it means to not be hungry all the time. This is very important. Some people report side effects in the first few months of treatment. The most common of these are nausea, abdominal pain , constipation, or diarrhea. This is quite logical, considering that the drug works by slowing down stomach emptying. If food stays in your stomach longer than you're used to, you may feel nauseated or uncomfortable. And if food passes through your stomach more slowly, it makes sense that you might notice changes in your bowel movements. For most people, these symptoms are most noticeable on the day they take the medication or when the dose is increased. Over time, they disappear on their own . In studies of GLP-1, most people reported that they experienced fewer side effects after the first few months of taking it . This is logical, as the usual GLP-1 regimen involves increasing the dose once a month for the first four months. When you reach a dose that you will take long-term, your body knows what to expect and, ideally , adapts, which means less nausea or constipation. So when someone asks you what it's like to take GLP-1, you can say: great.
